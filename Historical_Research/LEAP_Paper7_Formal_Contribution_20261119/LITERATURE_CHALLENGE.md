## Search routes and frontier

The search proceeded from 2026 SCP monitoring, generated documentation and software evolution (queries: `Science of Computer Programming 2026 stream-based monitoring RTLola parameter description generation`, `site:sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167642326000900 parameter description generation`) to 2025 SCP partial/best-effort monitoring and 2024 feature-to-code traceability. Outside SCP it searched the exact cited titles and synonyms including `monitor abstractions indistinguishable histories`, `functionality preserving input minimisation kernel OR`, `evolution-aware re-monitoring`, `pairwise behavioral change`, `code-comment consistency`, and `suppressed warnings`. The included manuscript/ledger provided the initial DOI leads; actual primary passages were reopened for pivotal claims.

**2026:** SCP's Chen et al. study asks how source context affects parameter-description generation; RTLola concerns stream specifications and monitor construction. ICSE FineMOP addresses change-impact selection for re-monitoring. None establishes a particular governance-alert decision rule for a generated-documentation revision. Their difference from Paper 7 is a research object distinction, not by itself a novel theorem.

**2025:** SCP's Ferrando/Cardoso partial-monitoring and Vamos middleware papers formalize different observability/monitoring constraints. iMOP and the suppression study directly establish software-evolution monitoring and warning-suppression phenomena in other venues. Paper 7's version-bound alert output is a narrower application.

**2024:** SCP's FMap paper addresses feature-to-code traceability. CoCC's publisher record shows online publication in 2024 and a 2025 issue; it assesses outdated comments from before/after features. Those papers support the relevance of changing source-derived descriptions, but do not establish that every changed generated description warrants warning revalidation.

**Older decisive foundations:** Henzinger et al. (2022) give equivalence-based monitor abstraction with indistinguishable prefixes. Antignac et al. (2017) directly order information projections by output kernels and use OR as an example where a joint input need not retain both bits. These sources defeat an unqualified claim that T1/T2 or two-channel OR compression is a new general theorem.
