Manuscript: Version Comparisons Preserve Governance Alert Decisions Across Evidence and Policy Revisions

Review type: Scientific, mathematical, methodological, literature, structural, and submission-quality assessment.
1. Scientific assessment
The manuscript establishes a coherent distinction between the existence of an active review episode and the warning displayed to a reviewer. This is the central contribution worth preserving.

Table 4 and Table 5 report that removing suppression-version binding changes 30 of 987 complete decision sequences and 48 of 5,286 individual event decisions, despite leaving all 987 active-episode sequences unchanged. The result directly supports evaluating displayed decisions separately from underlying episode activity.

The manuscript also develops a formal argument for replacing raw version identifiers with comparison indicators, retaining different amounts of information for decisions and diagnostic reasons.

The benchmark's reported design totals are arithmetically consistent: 329 temporal designs across five rule families and three representations produce 4,935 traces and 26,430 events.

The scientific question is substantive and the principal reported result supports it. The remaining review determines whether the theoretical contribution, source evidence, and final presentation satisfy the stronger Gold Niche submission gates.
2. Material findings requiring attention
M1 — The six-comparison representation is redundant
MAJOR theoretical refinement | Sections 4 and 7, pp. 10–11 and 18

Proposition 3 correctly proves that six Boolean comparisons preserve the complete decision and reason. The manuscript can establish a stronger result: five comparisons suffice under its own reason-selection rule.

The sufficient representation is:
\[
(\Delta_c,\Delta_p,O_c,O_p,S_{\mathrm{joint}})
\]

where the first two indicators identify current evidence and policy changes; the next two identify observation-context and observation-policy matches; and the final indicator records whether the suppression matches both current versions.

Equation (1) reports SUPPRESSION when suppression actually affects the display. It does not distinguish suppression-context mismatch from suppression-policy mismatch. The two separate suppression indicators can therefore be combined without losing any reported reason.

Required correction: Strengthen Proposition 3 or add a corollary establishing the five-comparison representation. Update the abstract, contribution statement and discussion to report this sharper sufficiency result. The three-comparison Proposition 4 remains valid.

This correction strengthens the theoretical contribution without changing the experiment or its reported results.
M2 — The notation for reset information is reused
MINOR mathematical clarity | Sections 3.2 and 4, pp. 8 and 10–11

The symbol \(r_i\) denotes the selected categorical reset reason in Equation (1), then becomes a Boolean version-change indicator in Proposition 4. These have different domains.

Required correction: Use distinct symbols, such as \(R_i\) for the reset-reason label and \(\Delta_i\) for the joint version-change indicator. Preserve the mathematical definitions and results.
M3 — Severity values are not fully specified in the manuscript
MAJOR methodological reporting | Table 1, p. 3; Sections 4–5, pp. 10–13

Severity is one of the three components of the primary decision tuple. The manuscript states that each rule family contributes its recovered severity, yet Table 1 identifies the five rules and lexical predicates without specifying their respective severity labels.

Required correction: Add the verified severity value for each rule family to Table 1, or provide an explicit mapping to the corresponding entries in the reproduction archive.

This closes a reporting gap in the definition of the primary endpoint. No new experiment is required.
M4 — The closest evolution-aware runtime-verification literature needs one update
MODERATE literature completeness | Section 2.4, p. 5

The manuscript cites Legunsen et al. (2019) on evolution-aware runtime verification. A directly related 2025 successor is:

Guan and Legunsen (2025), Instrumentation-Driven Evolution-Aware Runtime Verification, ICSE 2025.

The newer study evaluates change-aware monitoring across 2,028 revisions of 66 projects, concentrating on monitoring overhead and the detection of new violations. 

cs.cornell.edu

The distinction is relevant: Guan and Legunsen address efficient monitoring across program revisions; Paper 7 addresses whether version-bound evidence and suppressions preserve a specified decision and its reason.

Required correction: Add a concise comparison after the existing evolution-aware verification discussion. Establish the exact difference in research question and measured outcome, without attributing failures to the earlier researchers.

The missing study does not invalidate Paper 7's contribution.
